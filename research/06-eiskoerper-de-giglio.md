# Eiskoerper — De Giglio's Methodology

Stefano De Giglio's approach to gelato formulation, as described in *Gelato: Die grosse Eisschule & 75 perfekte Eiskreationen* (2019). [deGiglio2019]

---

## Core Philosophy: No Universal Base

The central insight of De Giglio's **Eiskoerper** ("ice body") concept: **every main ingredient deserves its own individually balanced formula.** A pistachio gelato should not use the same base as a strawberry sorbet or a chocolate gelato.

This is a departure from the traditional approach of making a universal "base bianca" (white base) and simply adding flavoring. De Giglio argues that a universal base:
- Compromises flavor intensity (too much dairy can mask delicate fruit, too much sugar can overwhelm nut flavors)
- Cannot optimize texture for each ingredient (chocolate needs different TS than mango)
- Forces one-size-fits-all sugar profiles when each ingredient benefits from specific sugar combinations

---

## The Five Pillars

De Giglio's formulation considers five fundamental components that must be balanced:

### 1. Zuckerarten (Sugar Types)

Different sugars are chosen based on the main ingredient:
- **Which sugars harmonize** with the flavor (sucrose for clean sweetness, dextrose for less cloying, honey for complexity)
- **POD and PAC targets** specific to this ingredient
- **DE values matter:** glucose syrups and maltodextrins at specific DE values are selected to fine-tune the balance

Example: A dark chocolate Eiskoerper might use more dextrose (less sweet, higher PAC) because the chocolate itself adds bitterness that balances the lower sweetness. A delicate vanilla Eiskoerper might use primarily sucrose for clean, transparent sweetness.

### 2. Fette (Fats)

Fat level and source tailored to the ingredient:
- **How much fat** complements rather than overwhelms the main flavor
- **Fat source:** cream fat (neutral), cocoa butter (melting point matters), nut fat (flavor contribution)
- Hazelnut paste at 15% of mix already provides ~9% fat — adding lots of cream would make it greasy

### 3. SLNG (Solidi del Latte Non Grassi)

Milk solids adjusted per ingredient:
- High SLNG supports creamy body in milk-based flavors
- Lower SLNG lets fruit flavors shine without dairy cloudiness
- SLNG = 0 for pure sorbets

### 4. Andere Feststoffe (Other Solids)

Non-sugar, non-fat, non-SLNG solids:
- Cocoa powder (adds TS without sweetness)
- Nut solids (protein, carbs, fiber)
- Fiber from fruit
- Stabilizers and emulsifiers
- Inulin or maltodextrin as body builders

### 5. Wasser (Water)

Water is what remains after all solids are accounted for:
- More water = more ice crystals = harder, icier
- Less water = denser, richer, smoother
- The water fraction determines how much freezes at serving temperature

## The Balance Sheet / Bilancio

De Giglio uses a **balance sheet** (Bilancio del Gelato) for every recipe. This is a table that tracks, for every ingredient:

## Sugar Selection Strategy

De Giglio emphasizes that sugar choice is not just about sweetness. Each sugar type has a character:

| Sugar | Character | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| Saccharose | Clean, transparent sweetness | Default sweetener. Clean flavors (vanilla, fior di latte). |
| Dextrose | Less sweet, cooling sensation | When main ingredient is already flavorful or bitter (chocolate, coffee). Raises PAC. |
| Glucose DE39-60 | Body, chewiness, mild sweetness | When you need TS and body without too much sweetness. |
| Glucose DE97 | Near-dextrose but in powder form | Convenient dextrose alternative. |
| Maltodextrin DE19 | Neutral, body builder | Boost TS in sorbets without sweetness. |
| Invertzucker | Prevents crystallization, moisture | Long-shelf-life products. Smoothness. |
| Trehalose | Half sweet, protein stabilizer | Delicate flavors. Protects during freeze-thaw. |
| Honig | Complex sweetness, floral | When honey flavor is desired. |
| Inulin | Not sweet, prebiotic, fat-like | Fat replacement. Body in reduced-fat recipes. |

**De Giglio's rule:** Start with sucrose as your base sweetener, then add other sugars to adjust POD and PAC independently. The goal is to match the sugar profile to the character of the main ingredient.

## Eiskoerper Categories

De Giglio defines different Eiskoerper types, each with its own approach:

### Cremige Eiskoerper (Creamy Ice Bodies)
- Cremeeis, Milcheis, Vanille, Fior di Latte
- Fat: 4-10%
- SLNG: 8-12%
- Sugar mix: primarily sucrose, some glucose/dextrose
- Stabilizer: LBG + guar + lecithin

### Fruchtige Eiskoerper (Fruity Ice Bodies)
- Sorbets, fruit gelato
- Fat: 0-4%
- SLNG: 0-6%
- Sugar mix: sucrose + dextrose + glucose (higher total sugar)
- Stabilizer: higher dosage, citrus fiber

### Nussige Eiskoerper (Nutty Ice Bodies)
- Pistazie, Haselnuss, Mandel
- Fat: 8-14% (from nut paste + cream)
- SLNG: 6-9% (less, because nut solids replace some SLNG function)
- Sugar mix: less total sugar (nut fat carries flavor)
- Nut paste: 15-20% of mix

### Schokoladige Eiskoerper (Chocolate Ice Bodies)
- Dark, milk, white chocolate; cocoa
- Fat: 6-12% (from cocoa butter + dairy)
- SLNG: 7-10%
- Sugar: often less than Cremeeis — chocolate itself contains sugar
- Cocoa solids count as "other solids" in the balance

### Spezial-Eiskoerper (Special Ice Bodies)
- Alkohol (lower freezing point: rum, amaretto)
- Kaffe (strong extract, minimal water addition)
- Joghurt (acidic, fermented)
- Karamell (cooked sugar: Maillard products, different POD)

## Overrun in the Eiskoerper

De Giglio considers air incorporation as part of the Eiskoerper. The "right" overrun depends on the ingredient:

- **Dense, rich flavors** (chocolate, hazelnut): lower overrun (25-30%) to maintain intensity
- **Light, fresh flavors** (lemon, yogurt): moderate overrun (30-40%) for refreshing character
- **Creamy flavors** (vanilla, fior di latte): moderate overrun (30-35%) for classic gelato body
- **Sorbets:** lowest overrun (15-25%) for clean, icy intensity

## Practical Application for the Calculator

The calculator should implement De Giglio's approach by:

1. **Starting with a recipe type** (Cremeeis, Milcheis, Sorbet, etc.) to set target ranges
2. **Selecting the main ingredient** (the star flavor) and calculating its contributions first
3. **Building the balance sheet** by adding supporting ingredients (dairy, sugars, stabilizers)
4. **Showing all parameters in real time** as ingredients are added/adjusted
5. **Highlighting out-of-range values** so the user can see what needs fixing
6. **Suggesting adjustments** (e.g., "PAC too low — consider replacing 20g sucrose with 20g dextrose")

The Eiskoerper philosophy means the app should have **different default templates/presets** for each type, not a single universal starting point.

---

## Key Takeaways

1. **No universal base** — each main ingredient gets its own formula
2. **Sugars are chosen for character**, not just sweetness
3. **DE values are a primary tuning tool** — glucose syrups and maltodextrins at different DE values control POD and PAC independently
4. **The balance sheet is the core tool** — track every parameter for every ingredient
5. **Fat, SLNG, sugars, other solids, water** are the five pillars to balance
6. **Overrun is not an afterthought** — it's part of the Eiskoerper
7. **SLNG (not MSNF)** — De Giglio uses Italian terminology consistently
