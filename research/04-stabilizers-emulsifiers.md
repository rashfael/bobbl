# Stabilizers & Emulsifiers / Stabilisatoren & Emulgatoren

Detailed guide to the stabilizers, emulsifiers, and functional fibers used in ice cream and gelato formulation. Focused on ingredients available in Germany. [goff2013], [deGiglio2019], [soukoulis2008]

---

## Why Stabilizers Matter

Without stabilizers, ice cream:
- Develops large ice crystals during storage (recrystallization)
- Has a coarse, icy texture
- Melts quickly and unevenly
- Separates (whey drainage)
- Lacks body and "chewiness"

Stabilizers are hydrocolloids — long-chain molecules that bind water, increase viscosity, and interfere with ice crystal growth. They don't prevent ice formation; they keep crystals small.

**Total stabilizer dosage:** 0.2-0.5% of total mix weight for gelato. Sorbets need the upper end (or more) because there's no fat or protein to help with structure.

## Stabilizer Profiles

### Johannisbrotkernmehl (Locust Bean Gum / LBG) — E410

**Source:** Seeds of the carob tree (Ceratonia siliqua), Mediterranean.

**Properties:**
- Galactomannan (mannose:galactose ratio ~4:1)
- Must be heated to 85 C for 20-30 minutes for full hydration
- Not soluble in cold water — forms lumps if added cold
- Once hydrated: excellent viscosity, smooth mouthfeel
- Best-in-class for inhibiting ice crystal growth [soukoulis2008]
- Synergistic with xanthan (forms gel-like structures at low concentrations)

**Dosage:** 0.1-0.3% of total mix

**Best for:** Premium gelato where you can heat the mix. The gold standard stabilizer.

**Practical tip:** Mix LBG with sugar before adding to hot liquid to prevent clumping. Or blend with other cold-soluble gums.

---

### Guarkernmehl (Guar Gum) — E412

**Source:** Seeds of guar beans (Cyamopsis tetragonoloba), India/Pakistan.

**Properties:**
- Galactomannan (mannose:galactose ratio ~2:1)
- Hydrates at lower temperatures than LBG (~60 C, partial even at room temp)
- 8x thickening power of cornstarch
- Good synergy with LBG (combined effect greater than sum of parts)
- Slightly less effective at ice crystal control than LBG alone

**Dosage:** 0.1-0.3% of total mix

**Best for:** When you want faster hydration or can't heat as high. Good in combination with LBG.

**Classic blend:** 2 parts guar : 4 parts LBG : 1 part carrageenan = standard industry blend at 0.15-0.3% total.

---

### Xanthan — E415

**Source:** Fermented polysaccharide (Xanthomonas campestris bacterium).

**Properties:**
- Cold-soluble — dissolves at room temperature
- Pseudoplastic (shear-thinning): thick when still, thin when stirred/scooped
- This is very useful: holds shape in the pan, but scoops easily and melts smooth on the tongue
- Excellent synergy with LBG — forms elastic gel-like structure at very low concentrations
- Stable across wide pH range (works well with acidic fruits)
- Can feel slightly "slimy" at higher concentrations

**Dosage:** 0.05-0.2% of total mix (powerful — use less than other gums)

**Best for:** Sorbets (cold-soluble, acid-stable), yogurt-based ice cream, simple preparations where you don't want to heat. Salt & Straw uses xanthan as their primary stabilizer.

**Practical tip:** Sprinkle into liquid while blending vigorously. Or pre-mix with sugar (1:5 ratio xanthan:sugar) to prevent clumping.

---

### Gummi arabicum (Gum Arabic) — E414

**Source:** Sap exudate of Acacia trees (A. senegal, A. seyal), Africa.

**Properties:**
- Cold-soluble
- Much lower viscosity than other gums — needs higher dosage
- Good emulsifying properties (unusual for a gum — most are purely thickeners)
- Provides a clean, light mouthfeel without heaviness
- Forms thin films around air bubbles and fat globules
- Does not gel

**Dosage:** 0.3-1.0% of total mix (higher than other gums due to lower viscosity)

**Best for:** Sorbets where you want clean fruit flavor without gummy texture. Emulsification without dairy proteins.

---

### Carrageen (Carrageenan) — E407

**Source:** Red seaweed (Chondrus crispus, Eucheuma, Kappaphycus).

**Properties:**
- Three types: kappa (firm gel), iota (soft gel), lambda (thickener, no gel)
- In ice cream: used at very low concentrations to prevent whey separation
- Prevents "wheying off" — the incompatibility between galactomannan gums and milk casein
- At >0.05% forms undesirable rigid gel
- Hydration temperature: ~70 C

**Dosage:** 0.01-0.03% of total mix (very low!)

**Best for:** Always in combination with LBG or guar in dairy-based ice cream. Never alone. Never in high amounts.

**Warning:** Over-dosing carrageenan makes ice cream rubbery/gummy. It's measured in hundredths of a percent.

---

### Tara-Gummi (Tara Gum) — E417

**Source:** Seeds of Caesalpinia spinosa tree, Peru.

**Properties:**
- Galactomannan, intermediate between LBG and guar
- Partially cold-soluble, full function at 80 C
- Similar ice crystal control to LBG
- Good synergy with xanthan
- Can be a direct LBG substitute

**Dosage:** 0.1-0.3% of total mix

**Best for:** Alternative to LBG. Sometimes cheaper or more available.

---

### CMC / Carboxymethylcellulose — E466

**Properties:**
- Cellulose derivative (chemically modified wood fiber)
- Cold-soluble — but must be pre-mixed dry with sugar to avoid clumping
- Good water binder, stable across pH range
- Common in industrial ice cream
- Less "natural" perception than plant gums

**Dosage:** 0.1-0.4% of total mix

**Best for:** Industrial formulations. Less common in artisan gelato.

## Functional Fibers

### Citrusfaser (Citrus Fiber)

**Source:** Processed citrus peel (orange, lemon).

**Properties:**
- Clean-label ingredient (no E-number needed in many markets)
- Excellent water binding capacity
- Adds body without gummy texture
- Improves melt resistance
- Cold-soluble (but better with brief heating)
- Contains pectin, cellulose, and hemicellulose

**Dosage:** 0.3-1.5% of total mix

**Best for:** Clean-label formulations. Sorbets where you want body without dairy. Combines well with other stabilizers.

**Practical tip:** Higher dosage than standard gums, but creates a more "natural" texture. Good as a partial replacement for gums.

---

### Inulin (as Body Builder)

**Source:** Chicory root (Cichorium intybus).

**Properties:**
- Long-chain fructo-oligosaccharide
- Forms a gel-like network when hydrated (especially HP/long-chain inulin)
- Mimics fat mouthfeel at 5-8% concentration [muszynski2020]
- At 5% addition, can replace ~50% of fat with similar texture
- HP inulin needs 65 C for full gel formation
- Prebiotic (gut health benefit)
- Minimal sweetness (POD ~8) and negligible PAC (~10)

**Dosage:** 2-8% of total mix (much higher than gums — it's a bulking agent)

**Best for:** Reduced-fat gelato. Adding body to sorbets. TS boosting without sweetness.

**Practical tip:** Dissolve in warm liquid (65 C+) for HP inulin. Native inulin dissolves at lower temperatures but has less gel strength.

## Emulsifiers

### Why Emulsifiers

Ice cream mix is an oil-in-water emulsion. Emulsifiers sit at the fat-water interface and control how fat behaves during churning and freezing:

1. **During churning:** Emulsifiers promote "partial coalescence" — fat globules stick together but don't fully merge. This creates a semi-solid fat network.
2. **This network:** stabilizes air cells, slows melting, improves scoopability, creates creamy mouthfeel.
3. **Without emulsifiers:** Fat stays as separate globules, air cells are unstable, texture is less smooth.

### Lecithin (Soja/Sonnenblume) — E322

**Source:** Soybean or sunflower (sunflower preferred for allergen-free).

**Properties:**
- Phospholipid — naturally amphiphilic (has both water-loving and fat-loving parts)
- Naturally present in egg yolk (~1.5 g per large yolk)
- Gentler emulsification than mono-/diglycerides
- Does not promote partial coalescence as strongly as E471
- Clean-label, natural

**Dosage:** 0.1-0.5% of total mix

**Best for:** Natural/clean-label formulations. When using with egg yolk in Cremeeis. Sorbets with some fat (e.g., coconut).

---

### Eigelb (Egg Yolk) — Natural Emulsifier

**Composition:** ~50% water, ~32% fat, ~16% protein, contains ~1.5 g lecithin per yolk.

**Properties:**
- The traditional emulsifier for Cremeeis and French-style ice cream
- Provides both emulsification (lecithin + proteins) and flavor (rich, custardy)
- Also contributes fat, protein, and color (beta-carotene)
- Must be pasteurized: heat mix to 83-85 C
- One yolk (~18 g) per ~200 g mix is a typical ratio

**Dosage:** 2-6% of total mix (50-100 g yolk per kg)

**Best for:** Cremeeis. When you want egg flavor as part of the profile.

**Practical tip:** Temper yolks (slowly add hot liquid to beaten yolks) to avoid scrambling. Heat to 83-85 C and hold briefly, then cool immediately.

---

### Mono- und Diglyceride (E471)

**Properties:**
- Most widely used industrial ice cream emulsifier
- Strongly promotes partial coalescence of fat
- Results in drier, firmer ice cream with better melt resistance
- Often combined with 20% Polysorbat 80 for synergistic effect
- Derived from hydrogenated fats — less "natural" perception

**Dosage:** 0.2-0.5% of total mix

**Best for:** When maximum texture control is needed. Industrial or semi-professional use.

## Stabilizer Blends / Mischungen

### Classic Industry Blend

By weight ratio: **4 LBG : 2 Guar : 1 Carrageen**

Total: 0.15-0.3% of mix

This is the proven standard for dairy-based gelato. LBG does the heavy lifting for texture, guar assists and hydrates faster, carrageen prevents whey separation.

### Simple Home / Artisan Blend

**Xanthan only:** 0.1-0.15% of mix

Simplest option. Cold-soluble, easy to use. Salt & Straw's approach. Works well but may have slightly gummy texture if over-dosed.

### Sorbet Blend

**Xanthan + Citrusfaser:** 0.1% xanthan + 0.5-1.0% citrus fiber

Or: **LBG + Guar + Gummi arabicum:** 0.1% LBG + 0.1% guar + 0.3% gum arabic

Sorbets need more stabilizer because there's no fat or protein to help. Citrus fiber and gum arabic add body without gumminess.

### De Giglio Approach

De Giglio uses specific stabilizer blends tailored to each Eiskoerper. His recipes often include:
- Johannisbrotkernmehl as the primary stabilizer
- Guarkernmehl as secondary
- Lecithin as emulsifier (instead of E471)
- Sometimes inulin for body

The exact blend varies by recipe type — this is part of the Eiskoerper philosophy of ingredient-specific formulation.

## Processing Notes

### Hydration Protocol

1. **Pre-mix dry:** Combine stabilizer powder with 5-10x its weight in sugar. This prevents clumping.
2. **Add to liquid:** Sprinkle the sugar/stabilizer mix into cold or warm liquid while stirring/blending vigorously.
3. **Heat:** Bring to target temperature for the stabilizer (see individual profiles above).
4. **Hold:** Keep at temperature for the required time (LBG: 20-30 min at 85 C).
5. **Cool:** Rapid cooling to 4 C.
6. **Age:** Rest in fridge for 4-24 hours. This allows:
   - Full hydration of all stabilizer molecules
   - Fat crystallization (needed for partial coalescence during churning)
   - Protein reorganization
   - Flavor melding

### Storage

All stabilizer powders should be stored:
- Cool, dry, sealed
- Away from strong odors (they absorb flavors)
- Use within 12-24 months for best results
- Gums can lose hydration capacity over time
