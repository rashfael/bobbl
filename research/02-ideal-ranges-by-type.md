# Ideal Ranges by Ice Cream Type / Zielwerte nach Eistyp

Target parameter ranges for the main categories of ice cream and gelato. These are guidelines — individual recipes may deviate based on the specific main ingredient and desired character. [deGiglio2019], [goff2013], [icecreamcalc]

## Cremeeis (Cream Ice / Custard Gelato)

The classic European "gelato alla crema". Egg yolk is a defining ingredient.

**Characteristics:**
- Rich, custardy flavor from egg yolks
- Full body from fat + protein (egg + dairy)
- Egg yolk acts as natural emulsifier — may not need additional emulsifiers

**Key ingredients:**
- Whole milk (base liquid)
- Cream 35% (fat source)
- Egg yolk (3-6 per kg, ~50-100 g per kg mix)
- Sucrose + glucose syrup (sweetening)
- SMP for SLNG boost if needed

**Balancing notes:**
- Egg yolk contributes ~32% fat, ~16% protein, ~50% water — account in calculations
- Each egg yolk (~18 g) adds ~5.8 g fat and ~2.9 g protein to the mix
- Higher fat from yolks means less cream needed
- POD should stay moderate (160-200) — egg flavor is best with moderate sweetness
- Traditional Cremeeis often uses only sucrose; modern versions mix with dextrose or glucose

**Example composition (per 1000 g mix):**
- Milk: 500 g
- Cream 35%: 150 g
- Sugar: 150 g
- Egg yolk: 80 g (~4-5 yolks)
- SMP: 30 g
- Stabilizer: 3 g

## Milcheis (Milk-Based Gelato)

Lighter than Cremeeis, milk is the star. No egg yolk required.

**Characteristics:**
- Clean, fresh dairy flavor
- Lighter body than Cremeeis
- More transparent flavor profile — flavors come through more clearly

**Key ingredients:**
- Whole milk (primary base)
- Cream 30-35% (moderate amount)
- SMP (SLNG booster)
- Sugars (sucrose + dextrose/glucose syrup)
- Stabilizer blend

**Balancing notes:**
- Lower fat means SLNG can go higher (9-12%) without excessive TS
- Needs good stabilizer blend for texture without fat's help
- Clean flavor profile makes this ideal for delicate flavors (vanilla, fior di latte)
- Without egg as emulsifier, consider lecithin or mono-/diglycerides

**Example composition (per 1000 g mix):**
- Milk: 600 g
- Cream 35%: 80 g
- Sugar: 130 g
- Dextrose: 30 g
- Glucose syrup DE60: 40 g
- SMP: 40 g
- Stabilizer: 3-4 g

## Fruchtsorbet (Fruit Sorbet)

No dairy. Fruit + sugar + water. The most challenging to balance because there's no fat or protein to help with texture.

**Characteristics:**
- Intense fruit flavor
- Clean, refreshing
- Slightly icy compared to cream-based (intended)
- Higher sugar content to compensate for lack of dairy

**Key ingredients:**
- Fruit puree or fresh fruit (25-50% of mix by weight)
- Sucrose (primary sweetener)
- Dextrose or glucose syrup (PAC adjustment)
- Water (if fruit puree isn't liquid enough)
- Stabilizer (higher dosage than dairy-based)
- Citrus fiber or inulin (body builder)

**Balancing notes:**
- Fruit brings water, sugars, acid, and fiber — all affect the balance
- Measured Brix of the fruit is critical — catalogue values are only a starting point
- Target total Brix: 28-32 for the finished mix (measured by refractometer)
- Higher POD (220-280) because no dairy fat or protein to carry flavor
- Higher PAC (280-340) needed for proper softness — sorbets freeze harder than gelato at same temp
- Acidic fruits (berries, citrus) need sugar adjustment — acid increases perceived sweetness
- No SLNG, no fat → stabilizers and fiber must do all the structural work
- Maltodextrin DE19 useful to boost TS without adding sweetness
- Inulin can add body and mimic some fat-like mouthfeel

**Example composition (per 1000 g mix):**
- Strawberry puree: 400 g
- Water: 250 g
- Sucrose: 180 g
- Dextrose: 30 g
- Glucose syrup DE60: 50 g
- Maltodextrin DE19: 20 g
- Stabilizer: 4-5 g
- Citrus fiber: 5 g

## Schokoladeneis (Chocolate Gelato)

Cocoa and/or chocolate add fat, solids, and flavor complexity.

**Characteristics:**
- Deep, rich chocolate flavor
- Higher TS due to cocoa solids
- Cocoa tannins add bitterness/astringency that balances sweetness
- Dark color

**Key ingredients:**
- Whole milk (base)
- Cream (moderate)
- Cocoa powder and/or dark chocolate
- Sugars (often less than other types — chocolate itself contains sugar)
- SMP

**Balancing notes:**
- Cocoa powder is ~95% solids — significantly raises TS
- Dark chocolate (70%) contributes ~42% fat and ~28% sugar — must be accounted in calculations
- When using dark chocolate, reduce cream (fat) and sugar accordingly
- Cocoa's bitterness allows slightly lower POD without tasting bland
- Cocoa powder (fat-free solids) absorbs water → can make mix thicker, may need less stabilizer
- Two approaches: cocoa powder only (more intense, less fat) or chocolate (rounder, more fat)
- Can combine both: chocolate for body + cocoa powder for intensity

**Example composition (per 1000 g mix):**
- Milk: 550 g
- Cream 35%: 80 g
- Dark chocolate 70%: 100 g (contributes 42 g fat, 28 g sugar)
- Cocoa powder 20-22%: 30 g
- Sugar: 100 g
- SMP: 30 g
- Stabilizer: 3 g

## Nusseis (Nut Gelato)

Nut pastes contribute high fat, protein, and solids. Pistachio, hazelnut, almond are classics.

**Characteristics:**
- Intense nut flavor
- Very high TS (nut pastes are ~95-97% solids)
- Rich, dense body
- Nut proteins aid emulsification

**Key ingredients:**
- Whole milk (base)
- Cream (moderate — nut paste already brings fat)
- Nut paste 100% (15-20% of mix)
- Sugars (slightly less — nut flavor needs room)
- SMP (less than Milcheis — nut paste provides solids)

**Balancing notes:**
- Nut paste at 15% of mix adds ~9% fat (for hazelnut) — reduce cream significantly
- Nut pastes are ~97% solids → TS can get too high easily, monitor closely
- Lower sugar % and POD — nut flavors are best with moderate sweetness
- Nut proteins contribute to emulsification — may need less emulsifier
- Nut fat has different crystallization behavior than milk fat — texture may differ slightly
- Nut pastes vary by brand and batch — check fat/protein content on packaging

**Example composition (per 1000 g mix):**
- Milk: 550 g
- Cream 35%: 50 g
- Hazelnut paste: 170 g (contributes 104 g fat, 22 g protein)
- Sugar: 120 g
- Dextrose: 20 g
- Glucose syrup DE60: 30 g
- SMP: 20 g
- Stabilizer: 3 g

## Joghurteis (Yogurt Gelato)

Tangy, fresh flavor from live yogurt cultures.

**Characteristics:**
- Distinctive tang from lactic acid
- Fresh, light taste
- Similar body to Milcheis

**Key ingredients:**
- Yogurt (natural, 3.5% or 10% — large portion of the base)
- Milk or cream (to adjust fat)
- Sugars
- SMP (if SLNG needs boosting)

**Balancing notes:**
- Yogurt is ~88% water, ~3.5% fat, ~8.5% SLNG — similar to milk but with lower lactose (partially fermented)
- Yogurt's acidity (pH ~4.0-4.5) increases perceived sweetness → can use slightly less sugar
- Don't overheat yogurt mix — kills cultures and changes flavor (some recipes add yogurt cold after pasteurizing the base)
- Acid can destabilize proteins → careful with stabilizer choice (xanthan handles acid well)
- Often combined with fruit (raspberry-yogurt, mango-yogurt)

**Example composition (per 1000 g mix):**
- Yogurt 3.5%: 500 g
- Milk: 200 g
- Cream 35%: 60 g
- Sugar: 130 g
- Glucose syrup DE60: 40 g
- SMP: 30 g
- Stabilizer: 3 g
