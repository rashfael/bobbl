# Salt & Straw Methodology

Tyler Malek's approach to ice cream formulation from *Salt & Straw Ice Cream Cookbook* (2019). An American craft ice cream perspective that contrasts with the Italian gelato tradition. [malek2019]

---

## Philosophy

Salt & Straw treats ice cream as a **creative medium** rather than a fixed formula. Their approach:
- Start with a reliable, well-tested base
- Use the base as a canvas for bold, experimental flavors
- Emphasize infusions, inclusions, and unexpected ingredient combinations
- Keep the base simple and consistent; let the flavoring do the talking

This is the opposite of De Giglio's Eiskoerper approach (ingredient-specific bases). S&S uses **one base for everything**, then adds flavoring.

---

## The Base Recipe

### Standard S&S Base (per ~1 L)

| Ingredient | Amount | % of mix |
|---|---|---|
| Whole milk | 330 g (1 1/3 cups) | ~40% |
| Heavy cream | 330 g (1 1/3 cups) | ~40% |
| Granulated sugar | 115 g (1/2 cup) | ~14% |
| Light corn syrup | 40 g (2 tbsp) | ~5% |
| Dry milk powder | 15 g (2 tbsp) | ~2% |
| Xanthan gum | 1 g (1/4 tsp) | ~0.12% |

**Total:** ~830 g base

### Approximate Composition

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Fat | ~17-18% (high — American ice cream style) |
| SLNG | ~7-8% |
| Total sugar | ~19% |
| Total solids | ~43-45% |
| Water | ~55-57% |

This is significantly higher in fat than Italian gelato (4-8%). It's a rich, American-style ice cream base.

## Key Design Decisions

### 1. Equal Milk and Cream

S&S uses equal volumes of milk and cream. This gives a base around 17-18% fat — towards the "super premium" end. The rationale:
- Fat carries flavor better than water
- Higher fat = smoother, more luscious texture
- Fat slows melting, keeping structure during the "eat from a cone" scenario

### 2. Xanthan as Primary Stabilizer

Unlike the Italian tradition (LBG + guar), S&S uses **xanthan gum alone**:
- Cold-soluble (no need for high-heat processing)
- Simple to use (just blend in)
- Pseudoplastic (scoops easily, holds shape)
- Only 0.12% — minimal amount

This simplicity makes the base accessible for home use. The trade-off: LBG is superior for long-term ice crystal control in storage. For ice cream that's eaten within days (as at S&S shops), xanthan is adequate.

### 3. Light Corn Syrup as Anti-Freeze

S&S uses light corn syrup (similar to Glukosesirup ~DE42) as their PAC booster:
- Keeps ice cream scoopable at low temperatures
- Adds body without excessive sweetness
- Prevents sugar crystallization

In European terms, this is equivalent to using a medium-DE glucose syrup.

### 4. Minimal Dry Milk Powder

Only 15 g (~2%) of SMP, compared to 3-5% in many gelato recipes. The high cream content already provides enough fat-based body that less SLNG is needed.

### 5. No Egg Yolk

S&S base is Philadelphia-style (no eggs), not French custard style. This gives:
- Cleaner, more transparent flavor profile
- Simpler processing (no tempering eggs)
- Lighter color (no yellow from yolks)
- Less emulsification — compensated by xanthan

## Processing Method

### Cook Minimally

S&S heats the base just enough to dissolve sugar — about 3 minutes at medium heat. This is much shorter than the traditional gelato process (85 C hold for pasteurization).

**Why minimal heating works for S&S:**
- No eggs to pasteurize
- Xanthan hydrates cold (no need to heat for stabilizer)
- In a commercial setting, they use pasteurized milk/cream already

**For home use:** Since you're using pasteurized dairy, a brief heat to dissolve sugar is sufficient. If using egg yolk or LBG, you'd need to heat to 85 C.

### Age the Base

S&S strongly advocates **aging** the base in the fridge for 4-24 hours before churning:
- Allows milk proteins to relax and reorganize
- Develops richer milk flavor ("cooked milk" notes mellow)
- Fat crystallizes into the right crystal structure for churning
- Xanthan fully hydrates

This aging step is universal across ice cream traditions — both Italian and American sources agree it's important.

## Flavor Strategy

### Infusions

S&S is famous for bold infusions into the base:
- **Steep**: Heat base, add flavoring (herbs, spices, tea), steep, strain
- **Blend**: Puree ingredients directly into the base (fruit, cookies, candy)
- **Swirl**: Add contrasting elements after churning (caramel, fudge, jam)

### The "3-2-1 Rule" for Inclusions

When adding chunks/swirls to ice cream:
- Add during the last 30 seconds of churning
- Pieces should be small enough to scoop but large enough to taste
- Layer swirls by alternating ice cream and swirl into the storage container

## Comparison: S&S vs. De Giglio / Italian Gelato

| Aspect | Salt & Straw | De Giglio / Italian |
|---|---|---|
| **Base philosophy** | One universal base | Ingredient-specific Eiskoerper |
| **Fat %** | 17-18% (super premium) | 4-8% (gelato), varies by type |
| **Stabilizer** | Xanthan only (0.12%) | LBG + guar + carrageenan blend |
| **Emulsifier** | None (no eggs, no E471) | Lecithin, egg yolk, or E471 |
| **Sugar types** | Sucrose + corn syrup | Multiple: sucrose, dextrose, glucose at various DE |
| **Processing** | Minimal heat, brief cook | 85 C pasteurization, structured process |
| **POD/PAC tuning** | Not explicitly calculated | Central to the methodology |
| **Overrun** | Higher (40-60%, commercial machine) | Lower (25-40%) |
| **Texture** | Rich, creamy, dense | Elastic, clean, intense |
| **Flavor focus** | Bold combinations, inclusions | Single-ingredient purity |

## Relevance for the Calculator

The S&S approach is useful as a **simple starting template** for users who want to:
- Start with a proven base and modify from there
- Use minimal specialized ingredients (no LBG, no glucose syrups)
- Make American-style ice cream rather than Italian gelato

The calculator could offer an "S&S-style" preset alongside De Giglio-style presets, with the S&S base as a fixed starting point and the ability to add flavorings on top.

### Adaptation for European Ingredients

S&S "light corn syrup" → Glukosesirup DE42-60 (readily available from Sueedzucker or ice cream suppliers)

The rest of the ingredients are standard European dairy.

---

## Key Takeaways

1. **One base, many flavors** — simpler than Eiskoerper but less optimized
2. **High fat** (~18%) — American premium style, not Italian gelato
3. **Xanthan only** — simplest stabilizer approach
4. **Minimal processing** — accessible for home cooks
5. **Aging is essential** — 4-24 hours in fridge before churning
6. **Creative freedom** — the base is a canvas, not a finished product
7. **Good starting point** for beginners, but De Giglio's approach produces more refined results for specific flavors
